What makes them premium (use ALL of these, every ad):
1. ONE headline, sentence case, two short lines, one weight; the second line in a tonal variant (cream→tan, white→mauve). Nothing else on the canvas except one tiny plain line. No bullets, no icons, no tiles, no badges, no italic accent word, no ALL CAPS shouting.
2. Product shot as an OBJECT, not a mockup: real material (walnut, linen, paper, stone, painted plaster), hard directional light, one long real shadow, confident crop (the product may exit the frame edge). Often top-down or straight-on. Never floating, never on a "marble ledge in a living room".
3. Monochrome harmony: the product's own colour family becomes the backdrop (tan canister on walnut; amber tube on amber; pastel pills on charcoal). For PT-141: cyan-on-cyan, glass-and-white on white, or charcoal with the cyan label as the only colour; a warm sand/terracotta field with the cyan as the one pop.
4. People: candid, mid-laugh, looking OFF camera, cropped tight, warm film grade, average-looking 40s–50s; the copy sits on a flat panel or wall beside them. Never a stock smile to camera, never a person "behind the product".
5. Copy tone: plain, lowercase energy, names the thing without a punchline. Tiny line = one fact (prescription, form, consultation).
6. Whitespace is the luxury: 60% of the canvas is empty field.
