## Compliance & claims
Standards 01
Global Hard NOs
These rules apply to every product and are listed only here. Product pages carry only their own product-specific Hard NOs. If a hook conflicts with a rule here, the rule wins.
Applies to every product
Creative messaging reference
Claims
- —No “clinically studied” or “clinically backed” for a compound. Only a named ingredient can carry that claim.
- —Never attribute an outcome to the compound. Lead with the mechanism, then the downstream benefit — “raises testosterone, and healthy testosterone levels support body composition.”
- —No body-composition or “fat and skinny” claims on any product. They cause rejections.
- —No targeted fat loss, and never name a body area — FTC rules prohibit it. No “melting belly fat” style claims.
- —Avoid “melt,” “burn,” “ripped” and “shred.”
- —No unsupported medical-style claims.
- —No exaggerated transformation claims.
- —No claims outside a product’s approved benefit scope.
- —Never call a sublingual all natural. Peptides support the body’s own production — that is the limit of the claim.
- —Say “supports,” never “builds.”
- —No “high circulation” — it doesn’t mean anything to the reader.
- —No vague phrasing that makes people infer — “one better night’s sleep,” not “one better night.”
Naming
- —GHK, never “GHK-Cu.” The suffix gets flagged.
- —Spell out “growth hormone” — never “GH.”
- —Compounded GLP-1s always carry the word “compounded” on labels and pages.
- —“GLP-1” is the umbrella term in headers and titles.
- —“Optima Nad+” — NAD+ listed first, B12 last.
- —No mention of needles, including “needle-free.” Say “easy sublingual delivery.”
AI disclosure
- —Every AI-generated image or video carries: “ADVERTISEMENT: AI-generated depiction and simulated portrayal. Not an actual patient or medical provider.”
- —The wording is “AI depictions,” never “AI deceptions.”
- —Disclosure is not optional. Nothing AI-generated goes live without it.
Proof & approval
- —Trustpilot always reads “Trustpilot rated 4.9 Excellent” — the same on every product. Never “Trustpilot rating” alone.
- —Every ad carries visible social proof: customer count, rating, or both.
- —Compliance questions route to Jeremy. Nothing goes live without his approval.
Rules current as of 11 September 2026. Flag any rule that is later reversed or superseded.

## Creative & page standards
Standards 02
Creative & page standards
How the work should look and behave. Composition, format and landing-page rules that apply to every product in this book.
Applies to every product
Creative messaging reference
Composition
- —The pill or tablet is the hero, not the bottle.
- —If a bottle appears, it is large, foreground and legible.
- —Product and benefit must be identifiable in under two seconds.
- —Product names run big. Make them memorable.
- —Texture and depth behind the product — never flat color.
- —No shadows or outlines on type.
Format
- —Bullet type stays readable at story size — set a minimum and keep it generous.
- —Don’t over-bullet. A simpler layout often performs better.
- —Creative must hold up in square and vertical alike.
- —Never end on a passive frame. The last thing they see is benefits and a clear, specific CTA.
- —The Everlife logo appears on every ad.
Imagery
- —Warm, sun-lit and candid. Real people, calm moments.
- —Pharmacy framing is a deliberate trust cue, especially for 45+.
- —Women’s creative: pinks, tans and beige. No oranges or greens.
- —Keep intimate-wellness creative tasteful — product-led, no bedroom scenes.
- —No generic stock-style couples.
Landing pages & pricing
- —Mobile first. Mobile is 95% of traffic.
- —Build from the approved template, then iterate.
- —No hamburger, no login, no links to other products on a traffic page.
- —Test variants structurally — A/B or multivariate, not ad hoc swaps.
- —Default to percent-off, not dollar prices, unless a price point is being tested.
- —Cap iterations at five per base concept. Variety wins.
Reference top-performing, lowest-CPA creative before generating new iterations.
