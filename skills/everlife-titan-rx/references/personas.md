# Persona critique — the target buyer reads the ad before Mitansh does

Run this on EVERY concept (image or video) after writing it and BEFORE the approval question. It is free: no tools, no credits.
Write the critique in the persona's voice, first person, 3–5 lines, timestamped for video (0:00–0:03 …) or top-to-bottom for a static.
Then list the fixes you made because of it. Show the critique under the concept so Mitansh sees what Mike saw.
(The personas speak in first person inside the critique only; ad copy never does.)

Both come from the bible's "Who it's for": men 40–65+ who want daily performance support without planning around a pill.

## Persona 1 — "Skeptical Mike", 52 (the main buyer)
Trains five mornings a week at a no-frills iron gym, runs a contracting business, married 25 years. Has tried an as-needed generic
pill from a telehealth site and hated planning the evening around it. Has seen a hundred gas-station "male enhancement" ads and
reads anything with flames, rockets or "MAX" as a scam. Scrolls Facebook at 9:30 PM on the couch next to his wife.

What Mike punishes (carried from the PT-141 Dave critique, 2026-10-28, rewritten for Titan-Rx):
- **Confusing what it is.** One product, ONE capsule, the Titan-Rx bottle. "Daily" must be obvious in 2 seconds: that is the reason
  he'd switch ("Once-Daily, No Timing Required").
- **Supplement-bro hype.** "Boost", "max", "beast mode", flames, rockets, a ripped 30-year-old: he scrolls past. Mature, strong,
  real, a man who looks like him at the gym. Never "rock-hard", never a body part.
- **Talking in circles.** "Performance" three times in five seconds reads like a robot dodging rules → one approved claim per beat,
  each a different idea; supporting lines never repeat a noun.
- **Jargon with no plain English.** "3-in-1 of what?" → the formula as a fact ("Tadalafil + Vardenafil + L-Arginine") once, and the
  mechanism in the bible's plain line ("Supports Blood Flow When It Counts"). Never one ingredient's claim unless Jeremy approved it.
- **Comparisons he's already making.** He thinks "is this Cialis?" → never name it or any brand; never "better than", never "ED".
- **A price he can't see.** The bible offer isn't verified yet, so no number: show the pharmacy trust (Doctor Prescribed, Ships Free
  From Pharmacy, 100,000+, Trustpilot rated 4.9 Excellent) and a clear CTA instead. Never invent a price or a discount.
- **The couch test.** Would he tilt the phone away from his wife? Then it fails: no bedroom, no bare torso, no wink-wink double meaning.

## Persona 2 — "Grounded Ray", 61 (the older buyer)
Retired firefighter, still lifts three days a week, grandkids on weekends, quietly noticed things aren't what they were. Trusts
doctors and pharmacies, distrusts influencers. Reads small type badly without glasses.

What Ray punishes:
- **Being talked down to or shamed.** "Real men…" style lines feel like a dig (that headline is ⚠). Prefer calm confidence:
  "Supports Confidence + Control", "Supports Everyday Readiness".
- **Small or busy type.** Headline huge, three short lines max, legible at story size (50+ rule).
- **A young model.** Talent 40–65 (grey, lines, real hands); a 30-year-old with abs loses him.
- **Medical-sounding promises.** "Treatment", "cure", "ED" anything reads like a TV drug spot without the doctor: never.
- **Hidden partner stuff.** The partner lines ("Be Ready When She Is Ready", "Good for You. Great for Her.") work only when the
  scene stays warm and clothed: a terrace dinner, a drive, a laugh in the kitchen. Never a bed.

## The rubric (score each 1–5; anything under 4 gets fixed before approval)
1. **Clear in 2 seconds:** what it is (one product, one daily capsule), who it's for (men 40–65+), the one promise.
2. **Plain English:** every claim word explained by the scene or a bible line; no jargon stack; no ingredient claim.
3. **Not scammy:** real product, real pharmacy proof, no hype words (boost, max, beast), no timing numbers, no invented offer.
4. **Couch-safe:** nothing Mike hides from his wife; nothing Ray finds crude.
5. **Worth a click:** the end card or last line answers "what do I do now" (the session's CTA) and "why trust it" (Doctor
   Prescribed, Trustpilot rated 4.9 Excellent, 100,000+).

Write the critique honestly. If Mike wouldn't click, say so and fix the concept before showing it.
