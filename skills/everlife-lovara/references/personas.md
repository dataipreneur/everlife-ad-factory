# Persona critique — the target buyer reads the ad before the team does

Run this on EVERY concept (image or video) after writing it and BEFORE the approval question. It is free: no tools, no credits.
Write the critique in the persona's voice, first person, 3–5 lines, timestamped for video (0:00–0:03 …) or top-to-bottom for a static.
Then list the fixes you made because of it. Show the critique under the concept.

## Persona 1 — "Careful Maya", 46 (the main buyer)
Scrolling Instagram at lunch. Noticed her desire and comfort changed over the last few years and quietly resents the "it's all in your
head" line. Allergic to cringe: anything that would embarrass her if a colleague glanced at her screen. Trusts a product that looks like
a pharmacy, not a nightclub.

What Maya punishes:
- Anything that blames her or sounds like a diagnosis ("Low libido?" lands as an accusation). Prefer warm, emotional bible lines:
  "From Distant to Deeply Connected", "Unlock Confidence, Desire & Connection".
- Bedroom imagery, lingerie, lips, a man looking at her, a couple. She wants the product and a woman like her, laughing, dressed.
- Explicit or innuendo copy ("Hits Deeper", "Crave"): fine in a group chat, not on her lunch-break screen.
- "For men" vibes: navy-and-chrome gym energy. Give her blush, tan, warm neutrals, white, soft teal and soft light.

## Persona 2 — "Plain-spoken Joan", 54 (post-menopause, skeptical of hype)
Reads Facebook on her iPad in the evening. Has tried "women's libido" gummies that did nothing and is wary of anything that sounds
like a supplement scam. Wants to know what it is, that a doctor prescribes it, and what it costs.

What Joan punishes:
- **Jargon stacks.** "3-in-1 blend… desire pathways… responsiveness" with nothing plain. → one approved claim per line, each a different
  idea; never stack synonyms ("intimacy support" ×3 is the trap).
- **Hype words.** "Exclusive", "Ignited", "Unlocked" without a reason. → prefer "Supports…" lines; ⚠ lines only with approval.
- **No proof, no price.** → the real bottle, Doctor Prescribed / Ships Free From Pharmacy (footer), "Save up to 45%".
- **Timing promises.** Any number of minutes from the old PMG ads. → never.

## The rubric (score each 1–5; anything under 4 gets fixed before approval)
1. **Clear in 2 seconds:** what it is (Lovara, for women), the one promise.
2. **Plain English:** every claim word is explained by the scene or a bible line; no jargon stack.
3. **Not scammy:** real product, real pharmacy proof, no hype words, no timing numbers.
4. **Screen-safe:** nothing Maya hides from a colleague, nothing Joan finds tacky.
5. **Worth a click:** the end card or last line answers "what do I do now" (a ✅ CTA) and "what's it cost" (Save up to 45%).

Write the critique honestly. If Maya or Joan wouldn't click, say so and fix the concept before showing it.
