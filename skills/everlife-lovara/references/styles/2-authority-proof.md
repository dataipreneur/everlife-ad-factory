# Category 2 — Authority & Proof

Library folders: Clinical-Research · Trustpilot Reviews · Open Letter · Article · Advertorial (boards 1, 2, 4, 6).
Why it works: pharmacy and clinician framing is a deliberate trust cue for 45+; women's intimacy is a category where trust matters more
than hype. Advertorials sit on **white** (bible + Justin's direction).

## 2A — Clinician moment
- **Looks like:** a calm woman clinician 40–60 in a bright consult room, shoulder-up, the bottle on the desk. ONE ✅ line ("Supports desire
  and sensitivity") and "Doctor prescribed, shipped free from the pharmacy".
- **Guardrails:** an AI clinician → the verbatim disclaimer covers it; no "clinically proven", no "treatment for low libido", no diagnosis.
- **Finishing:** `editorial` (video) or `finish_image.py --layout letter/article` (static).

## 2B — Review spotlight — ❌ GATED
- Needs screened, verbatim Lovara Trustpilot reviews (none on file). Never invent, paraphrase or borrow another product's review.

## 2C — Open letter
- **Looks like:** "Dear Everlife community," letter card beside the product; every sentence assembled from ✅ bible lines; signed "The Everlife team".

## 2D — Article (on white)
- **Looks like:** an editorial page, kicker "WOMEN'S INTIMATE WELLNESS", bible reference headlines ("From Distant to Deeply Connected"), a calm product
  still life (or, when people are approved, a candid woman shoulder-up), proof. No fake byline, no fake site menu.

## 2E — 3-in-1 explained
- **Looks like:** a clean, white advertorial card: "3-in-1 Female Sexual Wellness Blend" + three ✅ benefit rows, capsule hero.
- **Guardrails:** ingredient names only with their own approved claim, and only once Justin confirms the list; no mechanism science
  beyond the page.
