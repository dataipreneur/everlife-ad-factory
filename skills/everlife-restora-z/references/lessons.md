# Lessons already paid for (read every session)

What the creative lead rejected, and why:
- **Reusing lines across ads** ("Sleep is complex", "three established ingredients… one nighttime formula", "Sleep easier, sleep longer", "Explore Restora-Z"): "redundant… do better". Every ad gets its own angle, header, end card and CTA. Use the copy log.
- **Seedance "UGC"** (camera following a person, hands-only ASMR at 480p): "nowhere close to realistic UGC humans". Realistic humans → Marketing Studio UGC.
- **Creatify lip-sync on a still image** (Aurora a2v): a talking photo with studio TTS, no motion, stitched pauses. "Bad! even you know it." Never for UGC.
- **Split-screen layout / text boxes over faces:** "I need the whole 9:16 video!" Keep full-frame video and light, box-free overlays; Trustpilot goes at the bottom on a transparent background.
- **A weak storyline after a strong hook:** the hook has to pay off in a human story (e.g. the 3 a.m. hook → the husband brings HER pharmacy delivery the next morning).

What worked:
- The chaos → calm comedic hook (overhead hyper-lapse → same frame perfectly still). Capsule podcast characters. Exhausted sheep. Creator skit with odd POVs (inside the drawer). The partner filming "Who is this man?"
- The ingredient end card (each ingredient + its own claim), timed captions, the transparent proof row.

Generation traps:
- Selfie or front-camera prompts **mirror the bottle label** → `--mirror-until`.
- Marketing Studio may render a **second phone** at the bottom of the frame → say "the filming phone is never visible"; fix with `--zoom 1.2`.
- Doormats, mugs, book covers and clutter labels come out with **garbled fake text** → ask for plain or unbranded props.
- Two capsules tipped into a palm reads as a two-pill dose → "exactly ONE capsule".
- A phone flashlight under the chin rendered as a dark mask band across the face → ask for "soft even underlight, face fully visible".
- TTS / model voices say "Restoril" or "Restera" → write "res-TOR-uh zee" in the prompt (Creatify TTS: write "Restora Zee"); keep spoken drug names to ≤ 1.
- Whisper drops caption lines that also appear in `vocab`, and misspells the drug names → always hand-fix captions.
- Long scripts run under the end card → ≤ 35 spoken words, finishing by ~12 s.

Compliance traps:
- Rx sharing (a spouse handing over his own pills) → only the person's OWN pharmacy delivery.
- Reference ads in the PMG folder use $69 prices, "Asleep in 15 minutes", "Breaking:", first person, old proof numbers (4.8 / 50,000 / 70,000). Never copy those.
- Retired sublines must not sneak back in via a reused copy.json; build each copy.json fresh.


## Safe zones (2026-10-27)
- Meta 9:16 UI covers the top 144 px, the left/right 156 px, the bottom 535 px and the Reels profile notch (156–375 × 1216–1385). Copy, logo, proof, CTA and disclaimer all live in x 156–924 × y 144–1385. The old "Trustpilot bar at the very bottom" layout is retired.
- Cropping a 4:5 raw to 9:16 puts the footer on the product. Generate 9:16 natively with the COMPOSITION block, or outpaint.
- If the bottle cap rises above 30% height, the headline has nowhere to go: finish_modern refuses ("headline would touch the product"). Re-roll with the block; do not shrink the headline below legibility.
- GPT Image 2.5 on Creatify places the product LOWER than the COMPOSITION block asks (base ~60% instead of 52%). The fix that worked: one cheap reposition edit on the raw ("keep everything identical; make the bottle and capsule ~20% smaller and move them up so the base sits at 45% height; fill below with the same background"). Then, if it is still a few px tight, shift the photo up ≤50 px (crop the empty top, mirror-pad the bottom, which sits under the Reels UI anyway).
- finish_modern detects the capsule by full saturation (s>0.85). Blue satin or fabric highlights (~0.67) used to be mistaken for it and caused false "footer on product" refusals.

## 3D "Come Back Down" (2026-10-27) — REJECTED: "quality was trash and everything was just bad" — creative lead pinpointed: (1) looks fake/AI and low-res, (2) the surreal concept itself is wrong for this buyer.
- Surreal metaphor (bed floating over a city) at Seedance 480p read as cheap, obviously-AI footage. The hook was a distant wide shot: the man was a speck, no face, no human moment, nothing to feel.
- Continuity drift (the lamp changed design mid-shot), the headline auto-hid for a second and then sat on a bright lamp.
- Lesson: do NOT build cinematic/surreal ads from scratch. Start from the Drive ★ Winners and real human moments (see memory study-winners-first). If a Cinematic Big Idea is requested, keep the person close and expressive in the hook, keep the scene grounded (a real bedroom), use 720p for anything hero, and QA for "would a 50+ buyer believe this is filmed?" before showing it. Never describe a weak take as "strong".

## Image Batch Mode (2026-10-27): what finally worked
- Let GPT Image 2.5 design the WHOLE ad (layout, type, product, scene) with three refs: bottle, capsule and the type specimen (`assets/type_specimen.png`, imported in the Creatify project). With exact quoted strings + "render ONLY these strings" + minimum text sizes as % of canvas height, batch 1 came back 5/5 clean: labels right, text exact, big type.
- Footer = compact transparent strip over the calm bottom 18% (generate at the final ratio; the prompt keeps the bottom 18% empty). Old square raws get their bottom extended automatically.
- Run 4 in parallel (the Creatify per-project limit), queue the 5th as soon as one finishes. ~50 s per image, so a batch of 5 takes ~2 min.

## Video discipline (creative lead, 2026-10-27)
- A video costs 45–75 Higgsfield credits (= 45–75 Creatify images). Be precise: review the script AND the full prompt in depth, line by line, until satisfied, then review again, before offering "Approve & generate". Never send a first draft to the video model.
- Realistic humans talking/acting → Marketing Studio UGC (75 cr / 15 s, 720p). It is much better than the normal video model; Seedance humans were rejected. Seedance only for comedic, stylised or animated ideas.
- Prove the angle with a 1-credit static before spending on a video of it.
