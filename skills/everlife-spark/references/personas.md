# Persona critique — the target buyer reads the ad before Mitansh does

Run this on EVERY concept (image or video) after writing it and BEFORE the approval question. It is free: no tools, no credits.
Write the critique in the persona's voice, first person, 3–5 lines, timestamped for video (0:00–0:03 …) or top-to-bottom for a static.
Then list the fixes you made because of it. Show the critique under the concept so Mitansh sees what Dave saw.

## Persona 1 — "Skeptical Dave", 54 (the main buyer)
Scrolling Facebook on his phone at 10:30 PM next to his wife on the couch. Has tried generic sildenafil/tadalafil before, has been
burned by overhyped men's supplement ads, and has a hair-trigger BS detector for anything that feels fake or scammy. Buys for himself
and, quietly, for the two of them. Will not click anything he'd have to hide from his wife sitting next to him.

What Dave punishes (from his critique of the first PT-141 video, 2026-10-28; Spark has the same buyer):
- **Confusing what it is.** "So is it a nose spray or a pill under my tongue? I'm already confused." → ONE product, ONE tablet, the
  Spark bottle. The three ingredients are always named together ("PT-141 + Apomorphine + Tadalafil"), never one at a time.
- **Talking in circles.** "'Desire support' three times in five seconds. Did a robot write that? Sounds like gas-station hornet honey
  dodging FDA rules." → one approved claim per beat, each a different idea; never stack synonyms. Supporting lines must not repeat a noun.
- **Jargon with no plain English.** "Why does it say 'Brain-Based' and 'Non-Hormonal'? How is that different from BlueChew or Cialis?"
  → say what it means in the bible's own plain lines: "Non-Hormonal Desire Support" / "Supports Desire + Physical Response" / "3-in-1:
  PT-141 + Apomorphine + Tadalafil". Never name the competitors he's thinking of (Viagra, Cialis, BlueChew); never split the combo
  to explain it.
- **A cartoon selling a prescription.** "Zero idea why a cartoon is selling me a real prescription drug, or what it costs." → the real
  product render, the real pharmacy framing (Doctor Prescribed, Ships Free From Pharmacy), "Save up to 51%". For Spark the data
  disagrees with Dave: two of its three winning videos are 3D-animated couples (`winners/` V04, V06). Animation is fine when the
  offer and proof stay on screen and it lands on the real bottle + tablet; Dave's point that survives is "show the real product
  and the price".
- **Vague timing words.** "I have zero idea how fast 'on your timing' means." → "on demand" is the bible's word; show the moment
  (an unplanned Tuesday) rather than promise a speed. Never a number of minutes.
- **The sleazy test.** "Without some sleazy bikini clip I have to hide from my wife." → clothed, candid, daytime, shoulder-up. If Dave
  would tilt the phone away, it fails.

## Persona 2 — "Careful Maya", 46 (buys for herself; also the wife Dave buys for)
Scrolling Instagram at lunch. Noticed her desire changed over the last few years and quietly resents the "it's all in your head" line.
Wary of anything hormonal. Allergic to cringe: anything that would embarrass her if a colleague glanced at her screen. Trusts a product
that looks like a pharmacy, not a nightclub.

What Maya punishes:
- Any hint the ad blames her. Prefer the inclusive bible lines: "Desire Support for Men + Women", "Spontaneity, Back On".
- Bedroom imagery, lingerie, lips, a man looking at her in the ad. She wants the product and, if a person, someone like her, dressed.
- Hormone ambiguity: "Non-Hormonal Desire Support" early and plainly.
- An ED-only ad: Spark is for her too. The ⏳ ED headlines speak only to men; never more than one in a batch, and only with approval.

## The rubric (score each 1–5; anything under 4 gets fixed before approval)
1. **Clear in 2 seconds:** what it is (one product, one tablet), who it's for, the one promise.
2. **Plain English:** every claim word is explained by the scene or a bible line; no jargon stack.
3. **Not scammy:** real product, real pharmacy proof, no hype words, no timing numbers, nothing that sounds like a loophole.
4. **Couch-safe:** nothing Dave hides from his wife, nothing Maya hides from a colleague.
5. **Worth a click:** the end card or last line answers "what do I do now" (the session's approved CTA) and "what's it cost" (Save up to 51%).

Write the critique honestly. If Dave wouldn't click, say so and fix the concept before showing it.
