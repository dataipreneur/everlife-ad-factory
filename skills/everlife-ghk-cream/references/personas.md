# Persona critique — the target buyer reads the ad before the team does

Run this on EVERY concept (image or video) after writing it and BEFORE the approval question. It is free: no tools, no credits.
Write the critique in the persona's voice, first person, 3–5 lines, timestamped for video (0:00–0:03 …) or top-to-bottom for a static.
(First person is only the critique's voice: the ads themselves never use it.) Then list the fixes you made. Show the critique under the concept.

## Persona 1 — "Busy Dana", 41 (the core demo: the 40-year-old mom)
Two kids, a job, runs or does a class three mornings a week. Scrolls Instagram in the school pickup line. Has a bathroom shelf of
half-used serums and no patience for a 10-step routine. Wants one step that's worth it, from somewhere she trusts.

What Dana punishes:
- **Another routine.** Ten products, steps, layering charts. → one product, one daily step ("Your Daily GHK Glow", "Upgrade Your
  Skin Routine").
- **Influencer glam.** Ring lights, full makeup, a 25-year-old model, a gym-sculpted body. → a real woman her age, fresh-faced, morning
  light, lean and fit at most; never a bodybuilder.
- **Fear copy.** Anything that points at her face's "problems" (wrinkles, sagging, dark spots) or a before/after. → supportive
  "-looking" bible lines only.
- **Orange-and-green "clean beauty" clichés** (citrus slices, leaves): off-palette and generic. → blush, tan, beige, the navy-and-gold bottle.

## Persona 2 — "Skeptical Lisa", 53 (has tried the expensive creams)
Reads Facebook on her iPad at night. Spent real money on department-store creams that promised the moon. Knows "peptide" from the
skincare aisle and wants to know what this one is, that a doctor prescribes it, and what it costs.

What Lisa punishes:
- **Jargon stacks.** "Peptide… collagen… elastin… renewal… repair" in one breath. → one approved claim per line, each a different
  idea; one plain line saying what it is ("Peptide-Powered Skin Support").
- **Miracle promises.** "Years younger", "erase", "reverse", "clinically proven", a timeline. → never; ✅ "Supports…" lines only.
- **No proof, no price.** → the real bottle large and legible, Doctor Prescribed / Ships Free From Pharmacy (footer), "Save up to 75%".
- **Copper / chemistry talk.** → never "copper"; the name is "GHK Peptide Cream".

## The rubric (score each 1–5; anything under 4 gets fixed before approval)
1. **Clear in 2 seconds:** what it is (GHK Peptide Cream, a daily skin step), the one promise.
2. **Plain English:** every claim word is explained by the scene or a bible line; no jargon stack.
3. **Not scammy:** real product, real pharmacy proof, no miracle words, no before/after, no timelines.
4. **On-brand for her:** a real woman 30–55 (or the product alone), pinks/tans/beige, never glam or "huge", nothing that shames skin.
5. **Worth a click:** the end card or last line answers "what do I do now" (a ✅ CTA) and "what's it cost" (Save up to 75%).

Write the critique honestly. If Dana or Lisa wouldn't click, say so and fix the concept before showing it.
