# Category 2 — Authority & Proof

Library folders: Clinical-Research · Trustpilot Reviews · Open Letter · Article · Advertorial (boards 1, 2, 4, 6).
Why it works: pharmacy and clinician framing is a deliberate trust cue for 45+; skincare is a category full of hype, so a
doctor-prescribed cream from a pharmacy stands out. Advertorials sit on **white** (or warm cream).

## 2A — Clinician moment
- **Looks like:** a calm woman clinician 40–60 in a bright consult room (beige and white, no green scrubs), shoulder-up, the bottle on
  the desk. ONE ✅ line ("Supports collagen and elastin") and "Doctor prescribed, shipped free from the pharmacy".
- **Guardrails:** an AI clinician → the verbatim disclaimer covers it; no "clinically proven", no dermatology treatment, no skin exam,
  no diagnosis, no needles or aesthetic-clinic gear.
- **Finishing:** `editorial` (video) or `finish_image.py --layout letter/article` (static).

## 2B — Review spotlight — ❌ GATED
- Needs screened, verbatim GHK cream Trustpilot reviews (none on file). Never invent, paraphrase or borrow another product's review
  (Sermorelin GHK is a different product).

## 2C — Open letter
- **Looks like:** "Dear Everlife community," letter card beside the bottle; every sentence assembled from ✅ bible lines; signed "The Everlife team".

## 2D — Article (on white)
- **Looks like:** an editorial page, kicker "PEPTIDE SKIN SUPPORT", Tier A headline ("Firmer-Looking Skin Starts With Support"), a
  calm product still life (or, when people are approved, a candid woman shoulder-up), proof. No fake byline, no fake site menu.

## 2E — The peptide step, explained
- **Looks like:** a clean white advertorial card: "Peptide-Powered Skin Support" + three ✅ benefit rows, the bottle hero.
- **Guardrails:** GHK named only as in the bible lines; no molecule diagrams, no "copper", no mechanism science beyond the page.
