## Product facts for this skill
- **Product name on creative:** "PT-141". (The sublingual label prints "PT 141"; copy still writes "PT-141".) Ingredient: PT-141 (bremelanotide)
  — never name the generic in ads unless the brief asks; never "research chemical".
- **Two forms, both in scope (Mitansh, 2026-10-28):** **nasal spray** (10 mL) and **sublingual tablet** (2 mg, 8 tablets). A session picks the
  form (or "either"); the bottle in the picture shows it. **Copy may name the form** ("easy sublingual delivery", "nasal spray desire support");
  copy never states a strength, dose or count ("2 mg", "10 mL", "8 tablets").
- **Audience:** adults 30–55, men and women. Buyers are mostly men, some women, and many men buy for their wives. The skill asks
  Men / Women / Both and keeps tone and palette per audience (women → pinks, tans, beige; never orange or green).
- **Category convention (Mitansh):** ED-space ads typically feature an attractive woman, with many hooks, fonts and colours. Allowed here
  **within the gates**: tasteful, product-led, clothed, real-looking 30–55, candid; **no beds or bedroom scenes**, no body shots, no
  before/after, no couples in bed; **video framing stays shoulder-up**. The woman is a presence, not the pitch.
- **Language scope:** this page has NO "ED", "erectile", "performance" or "Viagra" language — do not import it from the Spark page or the
  live landing page (which says "Generic Viagra", "ignite", "hard-hitting", "instant": never copy). PT-141 is **desire** support: brain-based,
  non-hormonal, on-demand. "Supports", never "builds"/"boosts"; mechanism first ("Supports Central Desire Pathways" leads, then the benefit
  it serves). Never "arousal" or any form of it (bible Hard NO, 2026-11-02).
- **Offer:** "Save up to 23%" (percent-off, usable). No $ prices.
- **Disclaimer (verbatim):** `AI-generated depictions and simulated portrayals used for illustrative advertising purposes. Not actual patients or medical providers. Disclaimer: Individual results may vary. Doctor-prescribed compounded prescription.`
- **Landing page:** https://everlifemd.com/pt141/fb (last verified Aug. 28, 2026).

## The angle (agreed with Mitansh, 2026-10-28)
Everyone else sells the ability; PT-141 sells **the wanting**. Desire starts in the brain, not in blood flow, and it is there when
the moment is, not on a calendar. Men buy it for themselves and for their wives. Headlines make the reader feel the gap between
"could" and "want", or the freedom of an unplanned night: warm, a little knowing, never clinical, never crude. Lead with the strong
Tier A lines ("It Was Never Just Physical", "Some Nights Aren't Planned.", "Your Body Changed. Your Desire Doesn't Have to
Disappear.", "More Than Blood Flow", "Made for Moments, Not Calendars"), not the weak "Spark…" lines the old ads leaned on.
Tier B examples in the agreed register (PROPOSED, need approval): "The spark was never the problem. The signal was." ·
"Wanting is a brain thing." · "No plan. No problem." · "Still you. Still wanting."
