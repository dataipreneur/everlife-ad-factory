# Persona critique — the target buyer reads the ad before Mitansh does

Run this on EVERY concept (image or video) after writing it and BEFORE the approval question. It is free: no tools, no credits.
Write the critique in the persona's voice, first person, 3–5 lines, timestamped for video or top-to-bottom for a static.
Then list the fixes you made because of it. Show the critique under the concept so Mitansh sees what Marcus and Dana saw.

## Persona 1 — "Lifter Marcus", 41 (the Asana brief's buyer)
Bodybuilder-adjacent: six sessions a week, 230 lb, tattooed forearms, a chalk bucket in the garage. A shoulder that "talks", an elbow
from pressing, a knee that is "fine". Scrolls Instagram between sets. Has bought peptides online before and got burned by hype; reads
labels; calls it "BPC". Will not click anything that looks like a clinic, a miracle, or a guy who clearly doesn't lift.

What Marcus punishes:
- **Cure talk.** "Heals tendons", "fixes your knee", "anti-inflammatory" → "if it did that it'd be in a hospital, not an ad." Say
  "supports", name the tissue, stop. One quick-hit line per beat.
- **Jargon with no plain English.** "Connective tissue repair" needs "the stuff that holds the joint together" next to it, or a picture that shows it.
- **Soft imagery.** "That's a yoga ad." He wants the rack, the loaded bar, chalk, a body that has done the work. Hardcore is the
  compliment; a competition pose or a mirror selfie is the insult.
- **Vague speed.** "Recover faster" is a bible line, fine; "in days" or "overnight" is not. Never a number of days.
- **The sleaze test.** Needles, droppers, medical-looking vials being drawn from → he closes it. The vial sits on the bench like a water bottle.

## Persona 2 — "Pilates Dana", 44 (the women's list)
Pilates three times a week, lifts twice, walks the dog every morning. Her wrists and hips complain. Scrolls at lunch. Allergic to
"girlboss" copy and to anything orange. Trusts a product that looks like a pharmacy shelf, not a protein tub.

What Dana punishes:
- "Glow", "beauty sleep" and "skin" promises from a recovery product: "which is it?" → glow is a mood at most; the promise is joints,
  mobility, gut lining.
- Gym-bro aesthetics in her ad (black steel, neon, shouting caps) → soft light, blush/tan/beige, calm type.
- Anything that blames her body ("Is your recovery lagging?") → pronoun-free, matter-of-fact lines.

## The rubric (score each 1–5; anything under 4 gets fixed before approval)
1. **Clear in 2 seconds:** what it is (one form), who it's for, the one promise.
2. **Plain English:** every claim word is explained by the scene or a bible line; no jargon stack.
3. **Not scammy:** real product, real pharmacy proof, no cure words, no timing numbers, no needles.
4. **Looks like me:** for Marcus, a serious lifter in a serious gym; for Dana, strong and soft in a bright studio; nothing either
   would scroll past as "not for me".
5. **Worth a click:** the end card answers "what do I do now" (a bible CTA) and "what's it cost" (Take up to 40% Off).

Write the critique honestly. If Marcus wouldn't click, say so and fix the concept before showing it.
