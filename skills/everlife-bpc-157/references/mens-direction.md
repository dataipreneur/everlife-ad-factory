## Who he is (the product essence, in his words)
He trains four to six days a week and has for years. His body is the tool. Something always hurts a little: an elbow from pressing, a
shoulder from overhead work, a knee from squats, a hip from deadlifts. He is not injured, he is worn, and he refuses to lose a week. He
already does everything: sleep, protein, creatine, foam roller, sauna, ice bath, deload weeks. He has read about BPC-157 on forums and
podcasts as "the Wolverine peptide" (a nickname: NEVER in our copy; it implies healing and "Wolverine" is also an Everlife stack
product). He knows the serious form is the vial. He respects pharmacy, prescription and plain
language; he smells hype instantly. What he wants to hear: the recovery side of training finally gets support. Not "heals". "Supports."

Product truths that shape the picture: BPC-157 is a 15-amino-acid peptide sequence derived from a protective protein found in the
stomach (Body Protection Compound); the research behind it is mostly preclinical and focused on tendons, ligaments and the gut lining;
compounded Rx telehealth sells the vial as the main form. That is exactly why the copy stays at
"supports" and the imagery can be as hard as we like: the strength is in the man, the support is in the product.

## The look (use ALL of these in every men's ad)
1. **Cast:** a bodybuilder or powerlifter 30–45. Big, dense, veins, forearms, traps. Tattoos on arms, chest or neck are welcome.
   Beard or stubble, cropped hair, a scar is fine. Shirtless or in a stringer, cut-off tee, hoodie with the sleeves pushed up, lifting
   belt, wrist wraps, knee sleeves, chalked hands. Expression: between sets, breathing, jaw set, eyes to camera or down at the bar.
   Never posing like a competition, never a mirror selfie, never a smile-to-camera stock pose.
2. **Place:** a hardcore gym or garage gym. Black rubber flooring, a steel power rack, loaded barbell with iron plates (never a smith
   machine), dumbbell rack, chalk bucket, a bench with cracked vinyl, a torn poster, a fan, a window of hard daylight or sodium lamps.
   Alternatives: a cold car park after a session; a kitchen at 5 a.m. with a black coffee and the product; a boxing gym (bag, wraps).
3. **Light:** hard, directional, high contrast. One key light raking across the body; deep shadows; sweat and chalk catching highlights.
   Colour moods: charcoal + steel + chalk white; black + the vial's slate blue; black + red (the winners' red-black); walnut + brass
   for the "earned" quiet variant. No pastel, no clinical white, no neon unless it is the gym's own signage.
4. **Product staging:** the vial is a gym object: on the plate of a loaded bar, on the rack's J-hook, on the
   bench beside wraps and a shaker, in a chalked hand pushed toward the lens (the winners' "hand thrusts the bottle at camera" move).
   The vial is ALWAYS a still object: no syringe, pen, needle, droplets or injecting, ever. No capsules or pills anywhere.
5. **Type:** heavy condensed grotesque, all caps allowed for men ("BUILT TO BOUNCE BACK"), product name huge; or a stencil / industrial
   sans. Three-word power stacks work ("TRAIN. RECOVER. REPEAT." is Tier B; "Train Hard. Recover Smarter." is Tier A). Red, chalk
   white or slate accent. Three benefit pills with simple icons when the brief wants bullets; one line when it wants less text.
6. **Copy register:** plain, blunt, earned. No "bro" slang, no shouting about results. Hooks from the Men list: "For Bodies That Take a
   Beating", "The Body Takes Hits. Recover Accordingly.", "Heavy Lifts. Smart Recovery.", "For Lifters Who Feel Every Rep", "No Days
   Off Needs Smarter Recovery", "Your Foam Roller Isn't Enough", "Built to Bounce Back", "Recover Between the Sets".
